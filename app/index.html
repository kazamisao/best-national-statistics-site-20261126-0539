Fourteen regions, ranked

The five most populous regions — Khomas, Ohangwena, Omusati, Oshikoto and Erongo — hold 1,646,513 people, 54.5% of Namibia. The five largest by land, //Kharas, Hardap, Kunene, Omaheke and Otjozondjupa, cover most of the map and hold just over a fifth of its people.

02 Umur & Rumah Tangga — a young country, smaller homes

Namibia is young: seven in ten people are under 35, and more than a third are children. At the same time households are shrinking — 3.8 people on average, down from 4.4 in 2011 — while their number grew by almost two-thirds.

71.1%of the population is under 35
42.7%are children and teenagers, 0–17 (about 1.29 million)
21median age in years
51.2%women (1,548,177) to 48.8% men (1,474,224)
49%of households are headed by a woman
+62.7%households since 2011 — they grew faster than the population

Household size is not the same everywhere: Kavango West averages 5.7 people per home and Kavango East 5.5, while Erongo averages 3.2 and //Kharas 3.3. See the region profiles for every region's figure.

Source: NSA, 2023 Population and Housing Census Main Report.

03 Pendidikan — the schooling gap is a rural gap

Nine in ten Namibians aged 15 and over can read and write. But the chance of never having been to school at all depends sharply on where a child grows up: in towns it is one child in twenty; in the countryside, one in seven.

91.5%of people aged 15 and over are literate
5.2%of people aged 6+ in towns never attended school
14.4%of people aged 6+ in rural areas never attended school
2.8×a rural child is that much more likely never to have been schooled

The gap is the same story the maps tell: schools, like clinics and water points, must serve a population spread across 825,615 km², and the sparsest regions are the hardest to reach.

Source: NSA, 2023 Population and Housing Census Main Report.

04 Air & Perumahan — water reached further than toilets or power

Safe drinking water is the quiet success of the twelve years between censuses: nine in ten households have it now, up from eight in ten in 2011. Toilets and electricity lag far behind — and both lag worst in the countryside.

91.4%of households have safe drinking water (80% in 2011)
40.0%of households have no toilet — 302,385 homes
59.5%electricity access; 306,524 homes still without power
64%of rural homes have no toilet, against 20% in towns
78%of homes in Kavango West have no toilet; over 77% in Zambezi
47%of homes use mains electricity for lighting

Homes without power: 212,519 in rural areas and 94,005 in towns. The national energy compact counts the same census households.

Source: NSA, 2023 Population and Housing Census Main Report; Namibia National Energy Compact.

05 Kerja & Ekonomi — a small economy, a wide jobs gap

More than one in three Namibians of working age who want work cannot find it — and counting everyone who has stopped looking, more than half are out of work. The economy that must absorb them is worth US$12.35 billion, built on mining, services and tourism.

36.9%unemployed, strict definition (actively looking), 2023
54.8%unemployed on the broad measure, including discouraged workers
44.4%youth unemployment — the sharpest edge of the jobs gap
39.6%of women unemployed, against 34.6% of men
320,442people unemployed on the strict count
US$4,168GDP per person, 2023 (World Bank)

What the economy is made of

Mining was 18.0% of GDP in the third quarter of 2023; tourism contributes about 14.5% of GDP. Services dominate at 63.5%, farming, fishing and mining together at 20.6%, manufacturing and building at 15.9% (2024 shares).

Source: NSA, 2023 PHC Labour Force Report; World Bank World Development Indicators.

08 Tabel & Metode — all fourteen regions, and how to read them

Every number on this site, in one table. Click a column heading to sort. Figures are the final census counts; shares, household sizes and densities are computed from them and agree to one decimal.

Metode

  • Count. The census counted everyone present in Namibia on the reference night of 24 September 2023: 3,022,401 people in 756,339 private households. It was the country's fourth post-independence census and its first fully digital count.
  • Maps. The geographic map is schematic — region shapes are simplified, but each region sits where it sits. The cartogram keeps the same geography and sizes each circle by population, so area means people.
  • Unemployment. The strict rate counts people actively looking for work (36.9%); the broad rate adds those who have stopped looking (54.8%). Both come from the census labour-force module.
  • Areas. Regional areas follow the published administrative areas; the national total (825,615 km²) is the country's published figure, so the fourteen areas do not sum to it exactly.
  • Schooling. "Never attended school" covers people aged 6 and over. Literacy covers people aged 15 and over.
